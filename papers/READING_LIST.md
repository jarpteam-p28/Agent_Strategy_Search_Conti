### P1 · Do S&P500 Options Increase Market Volatility? Evidence from 0DTEs (2025)
*Greg Adams, Chukwuma Dim, Bjorn Eraker, Jean-Sebastien Fontaine, Chayawat Ornthanalai, Grigory Vilkov* · instruments: ES, S&P 500 index · access: open_pdf · testable on our data: partial

- **Why:** Complements the gamma-exposure study already in the method notes (video 04, where crossings continued) with an academic counter-view: net-positive dealer gamma makes hedging counter-directional (dealers trade against the move), which damps volatility and produces reversals in ES; negative gamma does the opposite. Structural WHY = execution mechanics of big players (delta-hedging by market makers) plus limited liquidity in the underlying. Tells us the regime switch: reversion when dealers are long gamma, continuation when short. Edge killers: change in dealer positioning (retail 0DTE selling flipping dealer gamma sign), CME/Cboe product changes. Decay risk: very new (2023-2025), so little crowding evidence yet. Related finding worth cross-checking in the same list: Cboe's paper 'gammasqueezes' estimates the maximum impact of dealer gamma on S&P volatility.
- **Should contain:** Intraday evidence that on days with expiring 0DTE contracts, 10-minute realized S&P 500 volatility is lower; that market makers' net gamma (built from option data) predicts lower volatility over the next ~10 minutes, fading after about an hour; and that higher net gamma predicts stronger order-flow REVERSAL and weaker momentum in E-mini futures. Needs SPX option volume/open interest to build gamma (we do not have it); the volatility/reversal behaviour on 0DTE-era vs pre-0DTE days can be tested on our ES data (ES ticks 2021-06 to 2023-06, 1-min to 2026-09; NQ ticks 2021-07 onward).
- **Get it:** Free PDF from author site: https://www.jean-sebastienfontaine.com/papers/0dte-options-volatility.pdf . Also SSRN abstract page https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5641974 -> 'Download This Paper'. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5641974

### P1 · Does overnight return predict the first half-hour return for U.S. market indices? (2025)
*Yasuhiro Iwanaga, Ryuta Sakemoto* · instruments: S&P 500 index ETF / U.S. market indices, ES, NQ · access: ssrn_free_download · testable on our data: yes

- **Why:** FINDING: A high overnight return negatively predicts the first half-hour return (intraday reversal) in U.S. indices. The effect is stronger in high-volatility periods and weakened after the 2010s. WHY: opposing clienteles. Retail buys overnight (pushing up the open) and arbitrageurs/daytime sellers lean against it. This is the overnight-vs-intraday tug of war applied to the first 30 minutes. It speaks directly to the opening range breakout and gap-fade debate: it says when to fade the gap and when not to. What would kill it: retail overnight demand fading or 24h market makers absorbing it. The paper itself notes the effect weakened after the 2010s, so decay is a real risk. Test it out of sample on 2021-2026.
- **Should contain:** Regressions and trading strategies of the first half-hour (09:30-10:00 ET) return on the prior overnight (close-to-open) return for U.S. market indices and ETFs. It should include subsample results by volatility regime (GFC, COVID, post-2010s weakening), a comparison with international index ETFs (where the effect is absent), and strategy performance. All of this can be rebuilt from 1-minute ES/NQ data. ES covers 2013-2026 and NQ covers 2021-2026.
- **Get it:** SSRN abstract page https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5807282 -> click 'Download This Paper' (check the button on the page; free SSRN download assumed, not confirmed in search results). https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5807282

### P1 · Gamma Fragility (2021)
*Andrea Barbon, Andrea Buraschi* · instruments: S&P 500 index, Nasdaq 100 index, ES, NQ · access: open_pdf · testable on our data: partial

- **Why:** Finding: intraday momentum shows up when dealers are net short gamma, and reversal when they are net long gamma. The effect is stronger when the underlying is less liquid. WHY (execution mechanics of big players / forced hedgers): option market makers must delta-hedge mechanically. Short-gamma dealers trade with the move and long-gamma dealers trade against it. The edge weakens if dealer positioning becomes less concentrated or if more non-dealer liquidity absorbs the hedging flow. This is the academic root of the gamma-exposure study on our desk (note 04). Useful for deciding when to apply continuation versus reversion rules on ES and NQ. The Baltussen et al. paper on hedging demand is already known, so this adds the intraday autocorrelation side and the stock-level evidence.
- **Should contain:** Builds a proxy for aggregate dealer gamma imbalance from options data, using a large panel of equity and index options. The sample includes S&P 500, Dow, Nasdaq 100 and Russell 2000 plus 276 stocks. Expect regressions of intraday 5-minute return autocorrelation on the sign of ex-ante gamma imbalance, and on the interaction with illiquidity. Also expect tables on the link to flash-crash frequency and size. Needs options open interest or gamma data, which we do not have. We can only test the price-side regime, for example intraday autocorrelation split by volatility or illiquidity proxies, on ES and NQ 1-min bars.
- **Get it:** SSRN abstract page https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3725454 -> 'Download This Paper'. A free PDF copy is on the University of St. Gallen repository (see pdf_url). A March 2021 version is at tashfeenomran.com/DB/Research Papers/Gamma Fragility.pdf. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3725454
- **Save as:** `papers/inbox/gamma_fragility.pdf`

### P1 · Market Return Around the Clock: A Puzzle (2022)
*Oleg Bondarenko, Dmitriy Muravyev* · instruments: ES · access: ssrn_free_download · testable on our data: yes

- **Why:** Directly extends the 'overnight drift' idea Conti uses but is not in our already-known list. Finding: the overnight premium is concentrated in a ~4-hour window around the European open (roughly 02:00-06:00 ET). Structural WHY (risk/liquidity premium + information accumulated while the US is closed): uncertainty builds during Asian hours and is resolved when European investors process it. That gives a precise clock-time window we can turn into a simple long-only 'European-open' ES rule and a 'different session' angle. What would kill it: 24h participation by US desks / Asian-session price discovery, or the VIX-uncertainty pattern vanishing. Decay risk: published 2020, so check the 2019-2026 out-of-sample portion of our ES data (our ES 1-min covers 2013-2026 in UTC; remember DST handling for European open).
- **Should contain:** Average E-mini S&P 500 excess return by time of day over a ~24h session, 2006-2018 (plus 2020 COVID check). Main claim: the four hours around the European open account for the entire average return, with a 1.6 Sharpe that stays high after costs and positive returns every year; the other 20 hours are a noisy zero. Also VIX-futures evidence (uncertainty rises overnight, falls at the European open). Needs only ES intraday bars, which we have.
- **Get it:** SSRN abstract page https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3596245 -> click 'Download This Paper' (70 pages, free with SSRN login). Published version: JFQA 58(2022/23). https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3596245

### P1 · Premium for Heightened Uncertainty: Explaining Pre-Announcement Market Returns (2021)
*Grace Xing Hu, Jun Pan, Jiang Wang, Haoxiang Zhu* · instruments: ES, S&P 500 index · access: open_pdf · testable on our data: partial

- **Why:** Finding: large positive overnight returns with no extra variance before NFP, ISM and GDP, similar to the pre-FOMC drift. About 36 announcements a year produce a pre-announcement return of roughly 3.4% a year. WHY (risk-premium / overnight pattern): investors are paid for holding through a period of rising uncertainty about how big the news impact will be, and the VIX tracks that uncertainty. The edge would fade if the uncertainty premium is arbitraged away or the announcement calendar changes, and the paper itself notes a weaker FOMC drift in recent years (decay risk). Worth testing as an ES overnight or time-window rule: long from the prior close to a few minutes before an 8:30 ET release, with a time exit before the release. Also test it on NQ from 2021.
- **Should contain:** Extends the pre-FOMC drift (already on our known list) to nonfarm payrolls, ISM and GDP. It uses S&P 500 index futures to measure returns from the close to just before the release, which is mostly the overnight session. Expect event-study tables of pre-announcement return by announcement type (FOMC about 27 bp, NFP, GDP and ISM smaller), with variance and VIX paths before and after. Needs release timestamps, which are public. Our ES 1-min ETH data covers the price side from 2013 on, but we would have to build the announcement calendar ourselves.
- **Get it:** NBER page https://www.nber.org/papers/w25817 -> 'Download PDF' (free). Free copies also at web.mit.edu/wangj/OldFiles/www/pap/HuPanWangZhu19.pdf and en.saif.sjtu.edu.cn/junpan/PreAnn_20210728.pdf. https://www.nber.org/papers/w25817

### P1 · The Derivative Payoff Bias (later retitled "The Third Friday Price Spike"; earlier version "The Equity Derivative Payoff Bias") (2023)
*Guido Baltussen, Julian Terstegge, Paul Whelan* · instruments: ES, S&P 500 index, SPX options (context) · access: ssrn_free_download · testable on our data: yes

- **Why:** FINDING: Equity prices drift up from Thursday close to Friday open on monthly option-expiry dates and then fully revert, because most index derivatives settle on the opening print. WHY: derivative settlement design plus intermediary hedging. Option market makers' charm-related delta hedging, or possible manipulation, happens in the illiquid overnight window just before the settlement price is set. This is a clean 'institutional constraint / execution mechanics' WHY. It maps onto the focus areas option expiry, futures roll/settlement and overnight session. It gives a calendar-based, rule-friendly pattern: long ES from Thursday RTH close into Friday 09:30 ET, then flat or fade after the open. What would kill it: moving settlement away from the AM open (the authors propose this), or dealers changing how they hedge. Decay risk: the paper is recent and covered by Bloomberg/Barron's, so it may be getting arbitraged.
- **Should contain:** Return profile of S&P 500 futures/SPX from the 3rd-Thursday close through the 3rd-Friday 9:30 ET open (the special opening quotation, SOQ) and the reversal by about noon. It should split the effect into quarterly (larger, roughly 26 bps) and off-quarterly expiries. It should also cover the pre-/post-2006 overnight-trading break, the 5-minute VWAP return construction, and the evidence on market-maker 'charm' hedging and manipulation. Data: intraday SPX/ES prices plus an option expiry calendar. It uses only index and futures prices, so it needs no extra data.
- **Get it:** SSRN abstract page https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4562800 -> click 'Download This Paper'. Free alternatives: AFA conference copy at https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=98196, or the paper link on Julian Terstegge's site (julianterstegge.com). ### P2 · Assessing the Profitability of Timely Opening Range Breakout on Index Futures Markets (2019)
*Yi-Cheng Tsai, Mu-En Wu, Jia-Hao Syu, Chin-Laung Lei, Chung-Shu Wu, Jan-Ming Ho, Chuan-Ju Wang* · instruments: ES, NQ, DJIA futures, HSI futures, TAIEX futures · access: open_pdf · testable on our data: yes

- **Why:** The only index-futures-specific ORB paper found, and it carries a structural WHY that fits the Conti method: the opening/closing minutes of the underlying cash market carry peak volume and volatility, and breakout signals line up with institutional order flow. We can replicate the idea on ES (1-min, 2013-2026) and test long-only vs both sides, volatility-scaled stops and time exits. Caveats: sample ends in 2013 (alpha-decay risk, check 2014+ out-of-sample), costs and slippage assumptions need checking, and the profits are strongest in Asian markets. A companion working paper by Holmberg, Lonnbark and Lundstrom (Finance Research Letters; crude oil futures only, profits concentrated in 2000-2011) is a cautionary read; a 2026 arXiv paper on MNQ ('Structural Limits of OHLCV-Based Intraday Momentum Signals in MNQ Futures', arXiv 2605.04004) tests ORB and gap signals and reports falsification results, useful as a sceptical benchmark.
- **Should contain:** One-minute data on DJIA, S&P 500, NASDAQ, HSI and TAIEX index futures, 2003-2013. Timely ORB (TORB): align the strategy's 'active hours' with when the underlying stock market is open, use a short opening probing window, and trade breakouts. Reports annual returns above 8% in all five markets (p-values below 3%), best probing time per market (short in the US, longer in Asia), and a TAIEX transaction-level check that TORB signals are in the same direction as institutional (especially foreign) traders.
- **Get it:** IEEE Access is open access: https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8641124/ -> click 'PDF'. DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2899177. Alternate PDF copies: the scispace link above, or the ResearchGate copy (CC BY-NC-ND). https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8641124/

### P2 · Nasdaq-100 Index Futures: Intraday Momentum or Reversal? (2005)
*Susana Yu, Joel Rentzler, Avner Wolf* · instruments: NQ, Nasdaq-100 futures · access: unknown · testable on our data: partial

- **Why:** Finding: NQ futures show both momentum and reversal in the opening hours, depending on the sign of the overnight and prior-day returns and on Mondays. The authors point to a structural feature of the futures market in the opening hours. WHY (information while the market is closed, digested on the open): overnight returns reverse after the cash open, while the prior day's return carries over. It is old and the effect size is uncertain, so treat it as a hypothesis generator, not a rule to copy. Its value is the conditioning grid (overnight sign x prior-day sign x weekday), which is cheap to test on NQ and ES, in-sample first. Expect the effect to have decayed since 2005, so a post-2021 test is a real out-of-sample check.
- **Should contain:** Multiple regressions of today's intraday NQ-futures returns on yesterday's return and last night's return. It conditions on the signs of those returns, bull versus bear markets, and day of week (Monday effects). Expect results by intraday period, including four opening-hour periods where the forecast model works. Last night's return is mainly associated with reversal, while yesterday's return gives both momentum and reversal. The sample is pre-2005, so it is an out-of-time check for us. Our NQ 1-min data starts in 2021-07, so we can re-test the same regressions on 2021-2026 (partial). ES can serve as a longer-history proxy.
