# Liquidity and Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift (Sadka 2006; Chordia, Goyal, Sadka, Sadka & Shivakumar 2009)

**Authors:** Ronnie Sadka, Tarun Chordia, Amit Goyal, Gil Sadka, Lakshmanan Shivakumar  
**Year:** 2009  **Type:** academic  **Read:** abstract_only  
**Source:** https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/l/liquidity-and-the-post-earnings-announcement-drift

> PEAD lives almost entirely in illiquid stocks — long-short PEAD earns 0.04%/month in the most liquid stocks vs 2.43% in the most illiquid (Chordia et al. 2009), and trading costs eat 70-100% of the paper profits; Sadka (2006) finds momentum and PEAD returns partly compensate for systematic liquidity (informed-trading) risk.

## Findings
- PEAD concentrated in highly illiquid stocks: value-weighted long-short 0.04%/month (most liquid) vs 2.43%/month (most illiquid). — *Chordia et al., Financial Analysts Journal 65(4), 2009.*
- Transaction costs account for 70-100% of the paper profits. — *Same paper.*
- Unexpected market-wide changes in the variable (informed-trading) component of price impact are priced in momentum and PEAD portfolio returns. — *Sadka, Journal of Financial Economics 80(2), 2006; intraday data 1983-2001.*

## The WHY
**Pattern:** Limits to arbitrage / liquidity risk: the drift survives where it is too costly to trade and partly pays for bearing liquidity risk.




## Setting
- Markets: US equities
- Instruments: stocks
- Period: 1983-2001 (Sadka); n/a (Chordia et al.)  Frequency: intraday / monthly  Holding: months

Liquid stocks 0.04%/month vs illiquid 2.43%/month; costs eat 70-100%.
