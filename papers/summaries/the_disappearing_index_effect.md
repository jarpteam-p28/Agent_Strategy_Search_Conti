# The Disappearing Index Effect

**Authors:** Robin Greenwood, Marco Sammon  
**Year:** 2025  **Type:** academic  **Read:** web_fetch  
**Source:** https://alphaarchitect.com/index-effect/

> The S&P 500 inclusion effect fell from +7.4% abnormal return in the 1990s to ~0.3% in the 2010s (deletions similar), as liquidity provision improved and most additions became mid-cap migrations.

## Findings
- Additions +7.4% (1990s) → +0.3% (2010s); deletions ~0.1% 2010-2020. — *Journal of Finance 80(2), 2025.*
- Similar declines in Russell and Nasdaq-100.

## The WHY
**Pattern:** Market adaptation to predictable index demand.




## Setting
- Markets: US equities
- Instruments: S&P 500 additions/deletions
- Period: 1990s-2020  Frequency: daily  Holding: days

## Reported results
The S&P 500 inclusion effect fell from +7.4% abnormal return in the 1990s to ~0.3% in the 2010s (deletions similar), as liquidity provision improved and most additions became mid-cap migrations.

## Tradable edge (our framing)
Stock-level; another example of a published flow effect decaying.
