# Paying Attention: Overnight Returns and the Hidden Cost of Buying at the Open

**Authors:** Henk Berkman, Paul D. Koch, Laura Tuttle, Ying Jenny Zhang  
**Year:** 2012  **Type:** academic  **Read:** full_pdf  
**Source:** https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu/bitstream/1808/10965/1/Koch_Paying%20Attention.pdf

> High-attention stocks rise overnight (+13 bp) and fall during the day (−13 bp) because retail buying at the open inflates opening prices; across all stocks opening prices sit ~8 bp above the prior closing midquote and fall during the first hour; the pattern tracks sentiment.

## Findings
- High-attention portfolio: overnight +13 bp, day −13 bp; hard-to-value stocks +43/−45 bp. — *3,000 largest US stocks 1996-2008.*
- All stocks: overnight ≈ +10 bp, day ≈ −7 bp; open ≈ 8 bp above prior close midquote; first-hour decline.
- Correlates with Baker-Wurgler sentiment (+0.30 overnight, −0.33 day). — *JFQA 47(4).*

## The WHY
**Pattern:** Retail attention-driven buying at the open.

- Markets: US equities
