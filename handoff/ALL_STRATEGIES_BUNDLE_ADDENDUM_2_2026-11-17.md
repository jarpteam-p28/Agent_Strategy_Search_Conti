### 32. `liquidity_recovery_long_es` — Liquidity-Recovery Long after a Stress Spike (ES/NQ daily, long only, time exit)
- **Spec:** `strategies/candidates/liquidity_recovery_long_es.yaml` · verdict: refine · data: yes
- **Finding:** Theory only, with no sample. Levered liquidity providers rely on collateralised funding. When volatility rises or they lose money, margins go up and capital shrinks, so they must cut positions and stop providing liquidity. Market liquidity then dries up across assets at once and prices overshoot fundamentals (loss and margin spirals). The paper's cited episodes are 1987, 1998 and 2007-08. It reports no trading statistics.
- **Why:** Institutional constraint / forced seller. Margin calls and VaR limits make deleveraging mechanical, so the overshoot comes from forced selling, not from information. Our framing differs from a price-drop rebound. We do not buy because price fell. We buy when there is evidence that the spiral has stopped, meaning price impact per unit of volume (our illiquidity proxy) has peaked and is falling. That is when the forced-out liquidity providers start coming back and the overshoot unwinds. Entering on the liquidity recovery rather than the price low is meant to avoid catching the falling knife while the spiral is still running.
- **Market:** instruments: ES; NQ; bar_size: 1min bars aggregated to daily RTH statistics; trades at RTH close; session: RTH 09:30-16:00 America/New_York (data stored in UTC) · direction: long_only
- **Entry long:** At the RTH close on day t, compute ILLIQ_d = (RTH high - RTH low)/RTH open divided by RTH volume, and smooth it with a 5-day average ILLIQ5. Stress state: ILLIQ5 was above the 90th percentile of its trailing 252-day distribution at some point in the last 10 sessions, AND ES is down at least 5% from its 20-day closing high. Trigger: the first close on which ILLIQ5 has fallen at least 25% from its peak in that stress window AND today's ILLIQ_d is below ILLIQ5. Buy 1 contract at the close.
- **Entry short:** —
- **Filters:** Only one position at a time; no new entry within 10 sessions of the last exit (cluster lockout, because one stress episode should give one trade); Warm-up: 252 sessions of ILLIQ history before the first trade; Volume: drop roll days, or use summed volume across contracts, so rollover does not fake a liquidity spike
- **Exit:** stop: Close below the stress-window low minus 0.5 x ATR(20) daily, checked on the close · target: None in the base test. Variant: exit at the 20-day close high that held before the stress · time: Exit at the RTH close 10 trading days after entry · other: Exit early if a new stress window opens (ILLIQ5 makes a new high above its previous peak), because the spiral has restarted
- **Parameters:** stress_percentile=90 (test 80, 85, 90, 95); illiq_smoothing_days=5 (test 3, 5, 10); recovery_drop_from_peak=0.25 (test 0.15-0.40); drawdown_from_high_filter=0.05 (test 0.03-0.10); hold_days=10 (test 3, 5, 10, 20); stop_atr_mult=0.5 (test 0-1.5)
- **Variants (pre-registered):** Intraday version: 30-min ILLIQ on 1-min bars, holding to the next close; NQ-leads-ES check: trigger on NQ ILLIQ, trade ES
- **Extra checks:** Compare with buying at the stress peak, and with buying after a plain 5% drawdown, to see whether the liquidity-recovery trigger adds anything; Compare with always-long ES over the same 10-day windows; List every trade with its date. Check whether the result rests on one episode (Mar 2020, Aug 2015, Feb 2018, 2022, Aug 2024) by dropping each in turn; Report pre- and post-2022 results; Report gross vs net (~$5 round turn ES); this matters less with multi-day holds; Check overlap with deleveraging_rebound_es_daily trade dates, because a high overlap means this adds nothing
- **Data needs:** ES 1-min RTH OHLCV 2013-12 to 2026-09 (have); NQ 1-min 2021-07 onward (have); Optional: VIX daily to cross-check the proxy (not in inventory)
- **Notes:** Read from the abstract only. All thresholds are our own constructs.; Chronological 80/20 split on 2014-2026 puts only about 2 stress episodes in out-of-sample. Treat the OOS result as weak evidence.

### 33. `liquidity_stress_overlay_es` — Liquidity-Stress Sizing Overlay on top of Vol Targeting (ES daily, falsification test)
- **Spec:** `strategies/candidates/liquidity_stress_overlay_es.yaml` · verdict: refine · data: yes
- **Finding:** Theory: during funding stress, liquidity collapses non-linearly and price moves become self-reinforcing. Volatility alone may underestimate tail risk while the spiral is running.
- **Why:** Forced deleveraging through margin and loss spirals. While illiquidity is high and still rising, liquidity providers are still being pushed out, so moves continue and gap. Cutting exposure in that state, beyond what volatility targeting already does, should reduce drawdowns.
- **Market:** instruments: ES; NQ; bar_size: daily (built from 1min); session: RTH 09:30-16:00 America/New_York; positions held close-to-close · direction: long_only
- **Entry long:** Baseline: hold long ES every day, sized by volatility targeting (20-day realised vol). Overlay: multiply that size by min(1, median_252(ILLIQ5)/ILLIQ5), and set it to 0 when ILLIQ5 is above its 95th percentile AND rising versus 5 days ago. Rebalance at each close.
- **Entry short:** —
- **Filters:** Apply the same overlay to the daily P&L of existing intraday strategies (e.g. vault_break, noise_area_momentum_es) as a second test
- **Exit:** stop: — · target: — · time: Daily rebalance at the RTH close · other: The overlay itself is the exit: size goes to 0 in the rising-stress state
- **Parameters:** vol_target_annual=0.15 (test 0.10-0.20); illiq_smoothing_days=5 (test 3, 5, 10); flat_percentile=95 (test 90, 95, 99)
- **Variants (pre-registered):** Conditional volatility targeting (Bongaerts et al. 2020): scale only when prior-month volatility is in its top quintile (cut) or bottom quintile (raise, cap 2x); unscaled otherwise.
- **Extra checks:** Use block bootstrap of daily returns (e.g. 20-day blocks) to keep stress clustering; Compare at equal average exposure; Check results in 2022, a down year
- **Data needs:** ES 1-min 2013-2026 (have); NQ 1-min 2021-2026 (have)
- **Notes:** This is a falsification test. A null result is useful because it would tell us volatility targeting is enough.

### 34. `overnight_persistence_index_es` — Overnight-Return Persistence on index futures: hold ES overnight only when recent overnights were strong (ES/NQ, falsification test)
- **Spec:** `strategies/candidates/overnight_persistence_index_es.yaml` · verdict: refine · data: yes
- **Finding:** In US single stocks (1992-2013), a stock's recent average close-to-open return predicts its overnight return over the next 1-4 weeks. The paper offers no index-level evidence. Our hypothesis is that the aggregate version (retail and attention flow arriving at the cash open in a run) makes the index's overnight drift persistent in time. If so, the overnight premium is concentrated in stretches that follow strong overnight runs.
- **Why:** Retail and attention flow is placed outside market hours and hits at the open. Retail imbalances persist for weeks, so overnight pressure should cluster in time. At index level this also overlaps with the overnight risk premium: holders are paid for carrying risk while the market is closed. Persistence would mean the overnight premium is time-varying and readable from its own recent history.
- **Market:** instruments: ES; NQ; bar_size: 1min; session: Hold from the 16:00 America/New_York close to the 09:30 America/New_York RTH open (ETH bars; data is in UTC, so convert with DST) · direction: long_only
- **Entry long:** At the 15:59 ET bar close, compute the average of the last N=5 overnight returns (RTH 16:00 close → next 09:30 open). If it is above the 60th percentile of its trailing 250-session distribution (signal_rule = 'top bucket'), buy 1 ES at the 16:00 ET close.
- **Entry short:** —
- **Filters:** Run no trade if the last 5 overnights include a session holiday gap longer than 3 days. Otherwise treat weekends as a single overnight.; Warm-up: the first 250 sessions are used for the percentile and do not trade.
- **Exit:** stop: Catastrophic stop at 2.0 × the 20-session average absolute overnight move, measured from entry and checked on 1-min ETH bars. · target: — · time: Sell at the 09:30 ET RTH open (open of the 09:30 bar). · other: One position per night.
- **Parameters:** lookback_overnights=5 (test 3, 5, 10, 20); entry_percentile=0.6 (test 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8; also simple sign > 0); percentile_window=250 (test 120, 250, 500); stop_multiple=2.0 (test 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, none)
- **Variants (pre-registered):** Inverse/reversal check: hold overnight only after weak trailing overnights.; Spread signal: use the trailing (overnight minus intraday) return gap as the sentiment proxy instead of the overnight level.; Pair with an intraday short of the RTH session after very strong trailing overnights (the Berkman give-back), but check overlap with overnight_reversal_first_hour_es.
- **Extra checks:** Compare against the unconditional overnight long and against the inverse rule (hold only after weak overnights). If the inverse rule wins, the index shows reversal, not persistence.; Same sign in every out-of-sample year; net t ≥ 2.; Down-year check: 2018 Q4, 2020, 2022.; Regression of the next overnight return on the trailing N-overnight mean, with HAC t-stat, before any rule tuning.
- **Data needs:** ES 1-min ETH/RTH 2013-2026 (have); NQ 1-min 2021-2026 (have); Exchange holiday calendar
- **Notes:** This is our own angle and not in the paper. Status is 'idea' until the regression shows any persistence.

### 35. `letf_anticipation_migration_es` — LETF Flow Anticipation Migration: earlier-afternoon entry, signed by the prior-close move (ES/NQ, long/short, flat at 15:59 ET)
- **Spec:** `strategies/candidates/letf_anticipation_migration_es.yaml` · verdict: refine · data: yes
- **Finding:** US stocks, June 2006 to Dec 2011. The 15:00-16:00 ET return moves with the leveraged/inverse ETF rebalancing flow implied by the index return from the prior close to 15:00. A 1% index day adds about 7bp in a large cap and about 13bp in financials and small caps. Part of the move reverses the next day. The effect was stronger in 2006-2009 and on up days. The author notes that anticipators front-run the flow, which pulls the price pressure earlier in the afternoon. Our existing LETF specs all enter at fixed late times (15:00 or 15:30). This spec asks a different question: since 2013, has the continuation moved earlier, to 13:30-14:30, as LETF assets and front-running have grown?
- **Why:** Execution mechanics plus a mandate. Daily-reset LETFs must trade r×δ×(δ−1)×AUM in the direction of the day's move near the close, and long and inverse funds add together. Everyone can compute the flow from the index move, so anticipators (prop desks, market makers pre-hedging) are likely to trade ahead of it. Their buying or selling is predictable, not forced. If they compete, the price pressure starts earlier and the 15:00-16:00 window already has part of it priced in. The forced flow sets the direction. Competition among anticipators sets the timing.
- **Market:** instruments: ES; NQ; bar_size: 1min; session: RTH 09:30-16:00 America/New_York (data stored in UTC; convert with DST) · direction: long_short
- **Entry long:** At clock time T ET (default 14:30), compute r = price at T / last trade at 16:00 ET the prior RTH day − 1. If r > k × σ20, where σ20 is the trailing 20-day standard deviation of close-to-close (16:00 ET) returns, buy 1 contract at the next bar's open.
- **Entry short:** Same rule, sell 1 contract if r < −k × σ20.
- **Filters:** One trade per day, with no re-entry.; Skip half-day sessions (early close at 13:00 ET) and days with missing bars around T.; Warm-up: require 20 completed sessions for σ20 before trading.; Mapping step (in-sample only): run the same rule for T ∈ {13:00, 13:30, 14:00, 14:30, 15:00, 15:30} and report gross points per trade for each T. Freeze one T before out-of-sample.
- **Exit:** stop: Master test: none, so the exit cannot flatter the entry. Live variant: stop at 0.3 × the 15-session average daily ATR from entry. · target: — · time: Exit at the 15:59 ET bar close (or MOC proxy at 16:00 ET) · other: Flatten all positions before the close and hold nothing overnight. The reversal leg is covered by letf_rebalance_day_reversal_nq.
- **Parameters:** entry_time_T_ET=14:30 (test 13:00, 13:30, 14:00, 14:30, 15:00, 15:30); k_threshold_sigma=1.0 (test 0 (always trade the sign), 0.5, 1.0, 1.5); absolute_threshold_alt=1.0% (test 0.5%, 0.75%, 1.0%, 1.5%); sigma_lookback_days=20 (test 10, 20, 60); stop_atr_fraction=0.3 (test 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, none)
- **Variants (pre-registered):** Long-only (up days only) per the paper's up/down asymmetry.; High-vol regime only: σ20 in the top tercile of its trailing 1-year distribution.; Combined with a VWAP filter: trade only if the price at T is on the same side of session VWAP as r.; Split the T→16:00 window into T→15:00 and 15:00→16:00 to see where the drift sits in each year.
- **Extra checks:** Chronological split: ES in-sample 2013-12 to about 2023-08, out-of-sample after that. NQ is short (2021+), so use it only as a confirmation instrument with frozen ES rules.; Choose T in-sample only. Prefer a plateau of neighbouring T values over the single best T.; Baseline comparison: unconditional long T→16:00 every day (zero-intelligence), and the existing 15:00 entry of letf_flow_to_depth_last_hour_es.; Report gross vs net points per trade, net t ≥ 2, and the same sign in every out-of-sample year.; Outlier dependence: share of profit from the top 20 trades.; Correlation of daily P&L with noise_area_last_half_hour_es and letf_flow_to_depth_last_hour_es. If above 0.7, treat it as the same strategy.
- **Data needs:** ES 1-min RTH 2013-12 to 2026-09 (have); NQ 1-min 2021-07 to 2026-09 (have); Exchange calendar of early-close days; Optional: VIX daily for regime split (not in inventory; σ20 from ES can substitute); Optional: LETF AUM history (SPXL/SPXU/UPRO/SPXS/TQQQ/SQQQ) to test whether the edge scales with AUM over time
- **Notes:** Not a copy of the paper's regression. This is a test of the timing claim (front-running moves the effect earlier) that our fixed-time LETF specs do not cover.; The expected edge is small, a few bp per day on the index. Costs will decide whether it survives.

### 36. `letf_relative_flow_spread_nq_es` — LETF Relative-Flow Close Spread: long NQ / short ES after up days, reverse after down days (15:30-16:00 ET)
- **Spec:** `strategies/candidates/letf_relative_flow_spread_nq_es.yaml` · verdict: refine · data: partial
- **Finding:** Cheng and Madhavan (2009) show that daily-reset leveraged and inverse ETFs must trade in the direction of the day's index return, by about (L²−L)×AUM×r. They do it near the close, and the impact is largest where that flow is big relative to closing liquidity. These numbers are from memory and have not been checked against the PDF. Our desk already tests outright late-day continuation on ES and NQ (letf_flow_to_depth_last_hour_es, letf_close_rebalance_continuation_nq, letf_anticipation_migration_es). The new angle here is cross-sectional between indices. Nasdaq-100 leveraged products (TQQQ, SQQQ, QLD, QID) carry far more 3x AUM relative to the index's closing depth than S&P 500 products (SPXL, UPRO, SPXU, SSO, SDS) do relative to the S&P's much deeper close. So on the same day move, the forced flow per unit of liquidity should be larger in NQ than in ES.
- **Why:** Execution mechanics plus a fixed mandate. The funds have no say over the size or timing of the rebalance: size comes from the prospectus and the day's return, and timing is the close. The flow scales with leverage squared and AUM, and its price impact scales with flow divided by closing liquidity. NQ's LETF complex is very large relative to its depth, so NQ should get a bigger same-direction push than ES. Going long one index and short the other cancels the shared parts of late-day drift: general intraday momentum, the last-half-hour market drift and index-wide hedging. What should remain is the part of the move explained by the difference in LETF flow-to-depth. That is a cleaner test of the WHY than any outright trade, and it also gives a return stream with low correlation to the outright late-day strategies.
- **Market:** instruments: NQ; ES; bar_size: 1min; session: RTH 09:30-16:00 America/New_York (data stored in UTC; convert with DST); universe: NQ and ES continuous front month, 2021-07 to 2026-09 (the period covered by both) · direction: long_short
- **Entry long:** At the 15:30 ET bar close, compute the day move r = NQ return from the prior RTH close (16:00 ET) to 15:30 ET. If r > +threshold: buy NQ and sell a beta-matched notional of ES (long the spread).
- **Entry short:** If r < −threshold at 15:30 ET: sell NQ and buy the beta-matched ES notional (short the spread).
- **Filters:** Absolute day move threshold |r| ≥ 1.0% (or ≥ k × 20-day average absolute close-to-15:30 return, to test the volatility-scaled version); Require ES's day move to have the same sign as NQ's, so the LETF flows in both indices point the same way and the spread isolates the difference in magnitude; Skip half-days (early close) and skip days with missing NQ or ES bars between 15:25 and 16:00; One spread trade per day
- **Exit:** stop: Spread adverse move > 2 × the 20-day average absolute 15:30-16:00 spread return (mainly a disaster stop; expect it to fire rarely) · target: — · time: Close both legs at 15:59 ET (variant: 16:00 settlement or 16:15 futures close) · other: Both legs are entered and exited together; never hold one leg alone
- **Parameters:** entry_time_et=15:30 (test 15:00, 15:30, 15:45); day_move_threshold_pct=1.0 (test 0 (sign only), 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0); hedge_beta_lookback_days=60 (test 20, 60, 120; also fixed dollar-neutral β=1); day_move_reference=prior RTH close (test prior close, RTH open); exit_time_et=15:59 (test 15:59, 16:00, 16:15)
- **Variants (pre-registered):** Explicit flow signal: (L²−L)×AUM×r ÷ 20-day average closing volume, computed per index; trade the sign of the difference; 15:45-16:00 window only; Outright NQ leg only, for comparison against letf_close_rebalance_continuation_nq
- **Extra checks:** Chronological 80/20 split on 2021-07 to 2026-09 (in-sample about 2021-07 to 2025-07); Bucket results by |day move| and by realized-volatility tercile; Pre/post 2022 and expiry-day vs non-expiry-day splits (0DTE confound); Long-spread vs short-spread sides reported separately; Correlation of daily P&L with letf_flow_to_depth_last_hour_es and noise_area_last_half_hour_es (target < 0.25); Sensitivity to hedge ratio (β vs dollar-neutral). If the edge exists only under one hedge choice, it is a beta artifact.
- **Data needs:** NQ 1-min continuous 2021-07 to 2026-09 (have); ES 1-min continuous (have); US market holiday/half-day calendar; Optional: daily AUM of TQQQ/SQQQ/QLD/QID and SPXL/UPRO/SPXU/SSO/SDS to build an explicit flow-to-depth ratio instead of the raw day move (not in inventory); Optional: closing-auction volume for QQQ/SPY constituents (not in inventory)
- **Notes:** Genuinely new relative to existing LETF specs: those trade outright direction, and this one isolates the cross-index difference in flow-to-depth; High decay risk: the paper is from 2009 and the closing-flow effect is well known; plan on half or less of any in-sample edge; Only about 5 years of overlapping data. With a 1% threshold, expect roughly 200-300 trades in total, so OOS will be thin; report confidence intervals
